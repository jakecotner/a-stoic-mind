For how is it not more in accordance with nature to be nourished from the earth, which is our nurse and mother, than from some other source? And how is it not more manly to live on the land than to sit in the city, as the sophists do? And how is living outdoors not more healthful than being reared in the shade? And what of this — is it not more befitting a free man to provide the necessities for himself than to receive them from others? For it is evident that not needing another for one's own requirements is far more dignified than needing him. So fine, so conducive to happiness, and so dear to the gods is a life lived from farming — provided one does not neglect nobility of character — that God proclaimed Myson of Chenae to be wise, and called Aglaus of Psophis happy, each of them living in the countryside, engaging in labor with his own hands, and keeping away from life in the town. Is it not, then, worth emulating and imitating these men, and cleaving with zeal to farming?

What then? Someone might perhaps say that it is a strange thing for a man capable of teaching and of leading young men forward into philosophy to work the land and to labor with his body like the country people. Yes, this would indeed be strange, if working the land prevented one from philosophizing or from helping others toward philosophy. But as it is, young men would seem to me to be benefited even more by not being with their teacher in the city, nor listening to him lecture in the school, but by seeing him at work on the farm and demonstrating in deed the very things that reason teaches: that one ought to labor and to endure hardship with the body rather than to depend on another for one's nourishment. And what is there to prevent the pupil, even while working, from at the same time hearing the teacher saying something about self-control, or justice, or endurance? For those who are to philosophize well do not need many words, nor must the young take in wholesale that crowd of theoretical points over which we see the sophists puffed up; for these are indeed enough to wear away a man's whole life.
