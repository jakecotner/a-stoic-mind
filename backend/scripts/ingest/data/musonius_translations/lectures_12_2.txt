For my part, I refrain from saying that everyone who does wrong at once also acts unjustly, even if against no one of his neighbors, yet certainly against himself, making himself worse and more dishonored; for the one who does wrong, insofar as he does wrong, becomes worse and more dishonored. So, to set aside the injustice, it is nevertheless wholly inevitable that self-indulgence belongs to the man who is overcome by shameful pleasure and takes delight in defiling himself, like swine. And of this sort, not least, is the man who consorts with his own serving-maid — something which some suppose to be, in a way, especially blameless, since every master seems to have full authority to use his own slave however he wishes. But to this my reply is simple: if it seems to someone not shameful nor out of place for a master to consort with his own serving-maid — and especially if she happens to be a widow — let him consider how it would appear to him if a mistress were to consort with her male slave. For surely it would not seem tolerable, not only if a woman who had a lawful husband should take a slave, but even if she did this while having no husband. And yet one will surely not think that men are worse than women, nor less able to school their own desires — men, who are stronger in judgment than the weaker, who are rulers than the ruled. For it is fitting that men be far better, if indeed they claim also to be set over women; whereas if they show themselves to be more without self-control . . . and worse. And that it is the work of lack of self-control and of nothing else for a master to consort with his serving-maid — what need is there even to say it? For it is well known.
